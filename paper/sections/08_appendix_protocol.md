# Pre-registered protocol and amendments {#sec:protocol}

**Freeze.** Hypotheses H1–H5, the confirmatory task sets, the model list with pinned serving providers, the grading code and the confirmatory analysis script were frozen on 2026-10-07 at 13:40 UTC, before any episode of the confirmatory sets was run. The freeze records a SHA-256 hash of every frozen file. The confirmatory sets are disjoint from the pilot and smoke sets: clinical `main` (100 tasks, 64 of them conditional), operations `main50` (a seeded stratified subsample of 50 tasks, six to seven per workflow template) and MLOps `main40` (a seeded stratified subsample of 40 cases by domain and oracle type; seed 20261007).

**Hypotheses as frozen.**

* **H1.** Among single-run regressions, the share the new model passes in run 2 or 3 exceeds 50%; pooled over pairs and testbeds, 95% CI from a task-level bootstrap (10,000 resamples); H1 holds if the lower bound exceeds 0.5.
* **H2.** At least one pair × testbed cell has a significant RNF count (within-task permutation test, 10,000 permutations, Holm across cells), and the pooled RNF rate among old-reliable tasks has a 95% upper bound below 10%.
* **H3.** Per pair, a two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test of per-task overreach rates (mean over three runs) on clinical tasks with an empty licensed set and on all operations tasks, Holm across pairs. H3 holds if at least two pairs change significantly *and* not all significant changes share a sign.
* **H4.** The procedure that re-runs each failure up to twice before flagging it costs at most 25% more episodes than a single run, on average.
* **H5.** In at least two pairs whose mean success improves (paired bootstrap CI above zero), the RNF count is significant.

**Exclusions.** Episodes lost to provider or API errors are not recorded and are re-run; a task enters a pair's analysis only when both models have all three runs. Grading uses only code.

**Amendment (2026-10-08, 11:12 UTC): H6.** After analysing the clinical confirmatory set, and before computing any flip or erosion statistic on the operations or MLOps data, we added H6, *upgrades erode reliability more than they break tasks*. Its statistic per cell is the net loss of always-passing tasks, #(old 3/3, new < 3/3) − #(new 3/3, old < 3/3), with a one-sided within-task permutation test (4,000 permutations, seed 0) and Holm across the cells of a testbed. Because it was formed on clinical data, H6 is exploratory on the clinical testbed and confirmatory on operations and MLOps, where it holds if at least two pairs show significant erosion in at least one of the two testbeds *and*, across all testbeds, erosion-significant cells outnumber RNF-significant cells. The amendment was written into the protocol and frozen together with the new analysis script.

**Freeze record.** Re-freezing for the amendment overwrote the original freeze file. Comparing file modification times shows that the only frozen files changed since the first freeze are the protocol text (the amendment) and the new transition-analysis script; all code, task sets, configurations and the confirmatory script are unchanged. Later freezes were written to dated files.

**Deviations during collection.** (i) Two Gemini 2.5 models were removed before the freeze because the provider no longer serves them to new accounts. (ii) Also during the pilot, before the freeze, two open-weight models were re-pinned to a different serving provider (one because its first provider returned routing errors on every call, one because a free-tier quota interrupted its runs); the affected episodes were quarantined, and none of them enter any analysis. (iii) The Gemini pair ran on a free tier whose quota turned out to be 50 requests per model per day; after the outage below it could not be completed and is excluded from all analyses. (iv) One model's provider rate-limited it during the re-run; after three fill passes 4 of its 100 clinical tasks lacked a complete set of runs and are excluded from that cell, as the frozen rule requires. (v) The protocol text stated that 49 clinical tasks have no licensed write; the frozen analysis code, which defines the set from the live reference solutions, finds 69, and all H3 results use the code's definition.

**Outage and enforcement of the exclusion rule (2026-10-08, 17:40 UTC).** An audit found that 2,077 of 3,300 clinical episodes had been graded while the record server refused connections (Section [-@sec:integrity]). These episodes fall under the frozen exclusion rule. They were quarantined, the harness was changed to check the server before each episode and to reject any episode containing a connection error, and the episodes were re-run with unchanged prompts, settings and response cache. The confirmatory analysis was then re-run unchanged. The analysis computed on the contaminated data is superseded and kept as an audit record. H6 had been formed on the contaminated clinical data; its confirmatory test on the operations and MLOps sets, which were unaffected, is unchanged.

**Budget.** Paid inference cost US$9.87 in total, including the re-run of the contaminated episodes, for which the approved cap was raised from $9.50 to $11.00 before any re-run episode was paid for.

**Pilot.** A 60-task clinical pilot, run before the freeze and unaffected by the outage, is reported only as exploratory (Appendix [-@sec:pilot]).

# Exploratory pilot {#sec:pilot}

Before the freeze we ran 60 clinical tasks (disjoint from the confirmatory set) three times with every model. In the pilot, several open-weight pairs showed significant reliable-negative-flip counts while the OpenAI pairs showed none, and unlicensed writes appeared to fall for some families and rise for others. These observations shaped H2, H3 and H5. None of them replicated on the confirmatory set: no cell is significant after correction, and unlicensed writes fall in every pair that changes significantly. We report the pilot to document how the hypotheses were formed, not as evidence.
